About Wills in Canada

Plain information to help you understand wills. This is general information only — it is not legal advice.

What is a will?

A will is a legal paper that says what you want done with your money, your home, and your belongings after you die. It also names the person who will look after your affairs. In Canada, wills are handled under the law of your province or territory.

Why having one matters

A will lets you decide who receives your things and who looks after your affairs. It makes a hard time easier for your family, because they are following your wishes instead of guessing. It can also name who would care for young children.

What an executor does

Your executor is the person your will names to carry out your wishes. Their job includes gathering your papers, paying any final bills and taxes, looking after your property, and passing your belongings on the way your will says. It is a job of trust, so most people choose someone organized who knows the family.

What happens if there is no will

If a person dies without a will, the law of their province or territory decides who gets their property and who looks after their affairs. Those rules may not match what the person would have wanted, and sorting it out can take longer and cost more for the family.

Where people usually keep one

People often keep their will at home in a safe place their executor knows about, with their lawyer, or in a safety deposit box. Wherever yours is, the important thing is that your executor knows where to find it.

If you don't have a will yet, a lawyer or notary in your province can help you make one. This page is information only — it is not legal advice, and this app does not make a will.
